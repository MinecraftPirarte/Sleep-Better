Why cutting sleep short is worse than it seems

When you cut a night short by one or two hours, you mostly lose sleep from the end of the night — which contains the most REM. A six-hour night doesn't give you 75% of an eight-hour night's benefit. It may give you less than half the REM sleep, the stage most directly tied to academic performance, emotional regulation, and memory.
